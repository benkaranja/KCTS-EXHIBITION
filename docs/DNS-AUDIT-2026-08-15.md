## 2. Must fix — mail is currently spoofable and Brevo cannot send

### 2.1 SPF authorises the old host and omits Brevo 🔴

```
current:  v=spf1 +a +mx include:_spf.truehostcloud.com include:zohomail.com ~all
target:   v=spf1 include:zohomail.com include:spf.brevo.com ~all
```

Three separate problems in one record:

- **`include:_spf.truehostcloud.com`** — your previous host is still authorised
  to send mail as your domain. Nothing of yours needs this.
- **`+a +mx`** — authorises whatever the domain's A and MX records point at.
  The A records are now Cloudflare's shared proxy IPs, and the MX records are
  Zoho's *inbound* servers. Neither sends your mail. `+a` in particular is
  effectively authorising a large shared IP pool.
- **Brevo is missing.** `include:spf.brevo.com` is not present, so every
  transactional mail the website sends will **fail SPF**. Combined with the
  DMARC policy below, that mail gets quarantined.

### 2.2 DMARC is still the old host's, and reports go to them 🔴

```
current:  v=DMARC1;p=quarantine;...;rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@truehostcloud.com;
                                     ruf=mailto:dmarc-reports@truehostcloud.com
target:   v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@kenyachinateasummit.com; fo=1
```

- **Your aggregate and forensic reports are being delivered to Truehost.** Those
  reports enumerate everyone sending mail as your domain. That is your data.
- **`p=quarantine` is live while SPF is broken.** Fix SPF *first*, then run
  `p=none` for about a week and read the reports, then tighten back to
  `quarantine` and eventually `reject`. Going straight to a strict policy on an
  unverified setup is how legitimate mail silently disappears.
- `aspf=s` (strict SPF alignment) is stricter than needed while two providers
  are sending. Relaxed alignment is fine until things are stable.

### 2.3 `mail.kenyachinateasummit.com` still exists 🟠

An A record pointing at Cloudflare's proxy — so `mail.` currently serves **the
website**. It is a leftover, it is confusing, and with `includeSubDomains` in
HSTS it is also a hostname you are promising to serve over valid HTTPS forever.
Delete it.

### 2.4 A third nameserver is still delegated at the registrar 🔴

```
COSMIN.NS.CLOUDFLARE.COM    ✅
ELOISE.NS.CLOUDFLARE.COM    ✅
NS3.CLOUDOON.ORG            ❌ remove at OwnRegistrar
```

Unchanged since the last report. A former host retains delegated authority for
your domain. This is the highest-severity item on the page and it cannot be
fixed in Cloudflare — it is a registrar change.

## 3. Recommended hardening

| Item | Why | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| **DNSSEC** | Not enabled. Signs your DNS so responses cannot be forged in transit. | Enable in Cloudflare, then add the DS record it gives you at OwnRegistrar. It is inert until the DS is added. |
| **CAA** | None set. Restricts which CAs may issue certificates for the domain. | **Must include `pki.goog`** — your certificates come from Google Trust Services. Omitting it will break renewal in November. |
| **www → apex redirect** | Three hostnames serve identical content. Canonical tags handle search engines, but a 301 is cleaner and removes the ambiguity entirely. | Cloudflare → Rules → Redirect Rules. Costs nothing on the Free plan. |

Suggested CAA set, given the current issuer:

```
0 issue "pki.goog; cansignhttpexchanges=yes"
0 issue "letsencrypt.org"
0 issuewild ";"
```

## 4. A decision you may not know you have made

Cloudflare is injecting a **managed robots.txt** ahead of the site's own. Live
right now:

```
Content-Signal: search=yes, ai-train=no, use=reference
Disallow: /   for GPTBot, ClaudeBot, CCBot, Google-Extended, Bytespider,
               Amazonbot, Applebot-Extended, meta-externalagent
```

So the site currently **allows search indexing but blocks AI training crawlers**.
That is a defensible default and arguably the right one for a client's content —
but it was switched on by Cloudflare, not chosen by you, and it has a trade-off:
blocking these crawlers reduces the chance the summit is cited in AI-generated
answers, which is a growing discovery channel for events.

It also means two `User-agent: *` groups are served. Valid, but untidy.

**No action needed — just be aware it is a setting, in Cloudflare → AI Crawl
Control, and that it is a business decision rather than a technical one.**

## 5. Fixed during this audit

- **Pages secrets were misconfigured.** The Turnstile *site key* had been pasted
  into the variable **name** field, so the production environment held one
  secret called `0x4AAAAAADr2iyH6s6QEYMuu` and nothing could read it. Replaced
  with correctly-named `BREVO_API_KEY` and `TURNSTILE_SECRET_KEY`; the malformed
  entry is deleted.
- **Turnstile site key wired into `project.config.json`**, so the widget now
  renders. It is public by design — it ships in the page HTML — which is why it
  belongs in committed config rather than a secret store. Verified live on
  `/registration/`.
- **`.env.brevo` and `.env.turnstile` consolidated into `.env.local`** and
  deleted. All three were already gitignored and **none had ever been
  committed** — verified against full history, so no key rotation is needed.
- **`domainAcquired` flipped**, so canonical, Open Graph, sitemap and robots
  moved to the apex. Verified live on all three hostnames.
- **Verified no private secret appears anywhere in `public/`.** Only the
  Turnstile site key and the sender display name, both public by design.
