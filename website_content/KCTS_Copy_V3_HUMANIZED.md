## One thing to know before you read

The humanizer skill wants personality: contractions, opinions, first person,
sentences that vary wildly in length, a bit of mess.

**I applied about two thirds of it.** The parts that fix AI tells — varied
rhythm, contractions, plain verbs, cutting the significance-inflation — all
apply cleanly to a summit site and make it warmer. The parts that inject
personal opinion do not. "I genuinely don't know how to feel about Kenya's
export concentration" is not a sentence the Tea Board of Kenya's partner
publishes.

So Draft B is Draft A with a pulse, not Draft A turned into a blog. If you want
it pushed further, say so and I'll take it another step.

## The difference in one paragraph

Draft A tells you things. Draft B talks to you. A says "Registration takes about
two minutes and does not commit you to attending." B says "Two minutes, and it
doesn't commit you to anything." Same fact, same honesty, different distance.

> The region most people picture when they think of Kenyan tea. About 260km
> northwest of Nairobi, up in the Rift Valley highlands. Big estates, and
> factories that take visitors.

## Kiambu and Limuru

> The closest tea country to the city, under an hour out. Short factory tours
> and tastings, which makes this the one to pick if you've only got half a day.

## Nandi Hills

> High-altitude estates in western Kenya. The tea has a different profile from
> Kericho's. Fewer visitors, longer drive.

## Nyeri and the Mount Kenya slopes

> Smallholder country on the southern slopes of Mount Kenya. Usually combined
> with Aberdare or Mount Kenya national parks.

## Not tea

> Nairobi has a national park inside the city limits, which is unusual enough to
> be worth a morning. The Maasai Mara, Amboseli and the coast are all a short
> domestic flight away.

# What actually changed, and my read on it

**Contractions throughout.** "Doesn't", "isn't", "we'll", "here's". This is the
single biggest difference and it's most of the warmth.

**Sentence length varies much more.** A runs a steady 15–20 words. B mixes
four-word sentences with 25-word ones. Read both aloud and B has a rhythm where
A has a metronome.

**The Secretariat speaks as "we".** A keeps a formal distance and says "the
Secretariat". B says "we'll email you". Warmer, and it makes the organisation
sound like people rather than an office.

**A few sentences carry a view.** "One of those numbers is a risk. The other is
an opportunity nobody has taken yet." "Selling a lot to very few buyers is a
weaker position than it looks." A states the facts and stops. B tells you what
they mean. That's the real editorial decision in front of you.

**Headings got shorter and more direct.** "Two per cent" instead of "The number
this summit exists to change". "What you're buying" instead of "What partnership
buys you".

## Where I'd land

**B for the homepage, About, and news. A for Venue, Travel, Media and the FAQ.**

The warmth earns its keep where you're persuading. On a page where someone is
checking whether they need a visa, or whether the venue has step-free access,
personality just gets in the way of the answer. Those readers want the fact, not
the voice.

Worth knowing: **A translates into Chinese noticeably better.** Contractions,
idiom and varied rhythm are exactly what machine translation handles worst. If
you pick B for English, the Chinese edition should still be generated from A's
phrasing, which means maintaining both. That's a real ongoing cost and it's the
strongest argument for picking A everywhere.

Nineteen `[TBA]` markers. Both drafts are as good as they can be while fees,
