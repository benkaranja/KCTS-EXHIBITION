## A commercial meeting point for Kenya and China

Tea connects Kenya and China in different but complementary ways.

Kenya is one of the world's major tea producers and exporters, with generations of knowledge in cultivation, processing and international trade. China is the world's largest tea-consuming nation, a leading tea producer and an important source of processing technology, investment and product innovation.

The Kenya-China Tea Summit has been created to bring these strengths into the same room. It is a platform for people who want to trade, invest, share expertise and build long-term relationships across the tea value chain.

The summit takes place in Nairobi from 21 to 23 April 2027.

## Why now

The commercial opportunity is changing.

China extended zero-tariff treatment to eligible imports from Kenya in May 2026. Kenya is also working to expand production of green, orthodox and specialty teas, improve value addition and reach a broader mix of export markets.

Tariffs are only one part of market access. Exporters still need to understand buyer preferences, product standards, food-safety requirements, packaging, documentation, logistics and distribution. Buyers need reliable suppliers who can deliver the right product, quality, volume and traceability.

The summit is designed to make those conversations easier and more direct.

### Improve market access

### Build lasting collaboration

### Share tea culture

## Featured insight

### Why the Kenya-China tea conversation matters now

Kenya and China already have a tea relationship, but its potential is much larger than current trade volumes suggest.

Kenya is a major producer and exporter with strong experience in black tea. The industry is also increasing its focus on orthodox, green, specialty and value-added products. China is the world's largest tea-consuming nation and has a highly developed market shaped by regional tastes, product knowledge, established domestic producers and demanding quality expectations.

In May 2026, China expanded zero-tariff treatment to imports from Kenya and other African countries with diplomatic relations with Beijing. The policy reduces one cost barrier, but it does not automatically create demand. Kenyan exporters still need products that fit the market, reliable buyer relationships, clear documentation, strong quality systems and effective distribution.

Recent trade figures show why those relationships matter. Kenya's tea exports to China fell to 1.22 million kilograms in the first quarter of 2026, down 51% from the same period in 2025. A quarterly decline does not define the long-term market, but it does show that access alone is not enough.

The opportunity is therefore broader than selling more bulk tea. It includes product development, processing technology, packaging, branding, direct sourcing, traceability, research and investment. It also requires a better understanding of what Chinese buyers and consumers value.

The Kenya-China Tea Summit is intended to bring those parts of the market together. Producers can hear directly from buyers. Technology providers can meet factories and growers. Investors can understand where capital is needed. Public institutions can address the standards and trade processes that shape commercial decisions.

The aim is practical: better information, stronger relationships and more credible routes from opportunity to business.

- Treat others with professionalism and respect.
- Be patient with language differences and communicate clearly.
- Respect personal boundaries and accept "no" without pressure.
- Ask before photographing, recording or sharing information about another person.
- Follow venue safety rules and reasonable instructions from authorised staff.
- Represent your identity, organisation and authority accurately.
- Keep commercial discussions honest and respect confidential information.

- Persistent contact after someone has declined
