## What this is

> The Kenya-China Tea Summit is an international meeting for the tea trade,
> hosted in Nairobi and centred on the commercial relationship between Kenya
> and China. It runs 21–23 April 2027 at the Kenyatta International Convention
> Centre (KICC).
>
> Orbitline Events & Ushers Ltd organises the summit, in partnership with the
> Tea Board of Kenya.

## Why now

> Kenya is one of the world's largest tea exporters. In 2025 it shipped 652.8
> million kilogrammes, up 9.81 per cent on the year before, earning
> KES 186.91 billion.
>
> Most of that volume reaches a small number of markets. Pakistan takes 36 per
> cent. China, the largest tea market on earth, took 13,011 tonnes in 2024,
> close to two per cent of Kenya's exports.
>
> The largest available market for Kenyan tea remains open. Reaching it takes
> buyers and sellers who have met.
>
> *Sources: [Tea Board of Kenya, Tea Industry Performance Report 2025](https://www.teaboard.or.ke/images/downloads/performance-highlights/TEA-INDUSTRY-PERFORMANCE-REPORT-2025.pdf); [Ecofin Agency, 2026](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-agriculture/1302-52890-kenya-tea-exports-rise-in-2025-revenue-slips-on-lower-prices).*

## What the summit works on

> **Trade.** Connecting Kenyan producers and exporters with Chinese importers,
> distributors and brands.
>
> **Value addition.** Specialty tea, green tea, orthodox tea, packaging,
> branding and product development.
>
> **Investment.** Bringing investors, banks and development finance institutions
> to commercially viable projects across the value chain.
>
> **Technology.** Putting processing equipment, agricultural technology,
> traceability systems and logistics in front of the people who use them.
>
> **Market access.** Bringing buyers, exporters, regulators and standards
> specialists into one conversation.
>
> **Culture.** Sharing Kenyan and Chinese tea traditions, consumer preferences
> and product stories.

## Theme

# Tea attractions in Kenya

**Target 250–350 · Draft 277**

> Kenya's tea regions sit close enough to Nairobi to visit either side of the
> summit. This page is orientation. The Secretariat's tour programme:
> **[TBA]**

## Kericho

> Kenya's best-known tea region, about 260km northwest of Nairobi in the Rift
> Valley highlands. Large estates, tea factories that receive visitors and the
> landscape most people picture when they think of Kenyan tea.

## Kiambu and Limuru

> The closest tea country to Nairobi, under an hour from the city. Estates here
> run short factory tours and tastings, which suits a half-day visit.

## Nandi Hills

> High-altitude estates in western Kenya, producing tea with a distinct profile
> from Kericho's. Fewer visitors and a longer drive.

## Nyeri and the Mount Kenya slopes

> Smallholder tea country on the southern slopes of Mount Kenya, often combined
> with a visit to Aberdare or Mount Kenya national parks.

## Beyond tea

> Nairobi has a national park inside the city boundary. The Maasai Mara,
> Amboseli and the coast are all reachable on domestic flights.

# Four negatives kept, and why

Everything else went. These four prevent a reader from making a real mistake,
which Part 3a of the skill treats as the exception rather than the rule.

1. **"rather than inside the building"** on Venue. An exhibitor who expects an
   indoor hall and finds a tent has a genuine problem on build day.
2. **"This is the Secretariat office"** on Contact. Without it, a delegate
   arrives in Westlands on the morning of Day 1.
3. **"The layout is subject to change"** wherever stands appear. This is
   commercial protection.
4. **"Requirements change. Check the official portal"** on Travel. Visa
   guidance that reads as authoritative and turns out to be stale is the one
   error on this site that stops someone boarding a plane.

# One FAQ removed

*"Is this the first Kenya-China Tea Summit?"* is gone. The honest answer was a
statement about what the Secretariat has not confirmed, which no confident site
publishes. Raising the question and declining to answer it was worse than not
raising it. Nothing on the site claims or denies a first edition, so FACTS.md
§2 still holds.

If the Secretariat confirms in writing that 2027 is the first edition, this
becomes a strong sentence on the Partner page rather than an FAQ entry.
